# Questions for your aunt — third user profile

Ask these in order, out loud, and write down her answers in her own words.
Do not paraphrase. Her exact phrasing is the evidence.

Record on a phone if she agrees — it is easier than writing while listening.

---

## Part 1 — First, the screening question

> **"Do you take any medicine every day, or see the same doctor regularly?"**

Her answer decides your path:

- **Yes** → continue with Part 2A
- **No** → skip to Part 2B

---

## Part 2A — If she has an ongoing condition

This becomes your strongest profile. Ask all six.

**1.** What condition do you see a doctor for, and how often do you go?

**2.** Do you always see the same doctor, or different ones each visit?

**3.** Which hospital or clinic do you go to, and what insurance does it take?

**4.** **If your insurance changed tomorrow, would you know whether your doctor is still covered?**

**5.** If your doctor was not covered anymore, what would you do — find a new doctor, or pay yourself?

**6.** Has a hospital or clinic ever told you at the desk that your insurance was not accepted there? What happened?

**6b.** Do you need approval from the insurance company before your appointments, and who arranges it?

---

## Part 2B — If she has no ongoing condition

Ask these six instead. They are close to what your mum answered, but
phrased to expose anything different about her situation.

**1.** What insurance company are you on, and what class is written on your card — Class A or Class B?

**2.** Have you changed jobs or insurance plans in the last two years?

**3.** When you or your family needed a hospital or a specialist, did you know which one to go to?

**4.** **How do you find out which hospitals accept your insurance?**

**5.** Is there any one place — website, app, phone number — where you can check which hospitals your class covers?

**6.** Has a hospital ever turned you away, or told you your insurance was not accepted? What did you do?

---

## Part 3 — Ask every aunt, whichever path

These two are the ones that produce quotable lines.

**7.** **If you needed to know tonight whether a hospital takes your insurance, what would you do?**

**8.** What would make this easier for you — a website, an app, a phone call, or asking at the hospital?

---

## After the interview

Write down, in three lines:

1. **Who she is** — age band, city, insurance company, class. No full name.
2. **What she needs** — the question she would open an app to ask.
3. **What the app must consider** — the constraint that changes the answer.

Then **test it**: type her question into Insurance Buddy and see whether the
answer differs from what your mum would get. If the answers are identical, the
profile is not distinct — that is a finding, not a failure, and worth writing down.

---

## Notes for you

- Do not lead her. Ask, then stay quiet. Silence gets better answers than suggestions.
- If she says "I don't know", that is a real answer and a useful one. Write it down.
- Do not correct her understanding of insurance. Record the misunderstanding —
  it is exactly the problem your project claims to solve.
- Keep her name out of the PDF. "A 50-year-old in Riyadh" is enough.
