### What the SME program promises, in Malath's words

Malath's own text says the program:

- has "a various number of **plans and classes** which are available to suit the choice of yours";
- covers "all of Saudi and Non-Saudi employees and their families";
- offers "additional upgraded benefits for **higher classes** as per employer's interests";
- includes instant quotation, instant issuance with cards, CCHI upload, online medical approval,
  SMS notifications, and reimbursement by bank transfer.

Benefits are described generically (in-patient / out-patient / comprehensive) with no tier table.

## The "classes" wording — handle with care

Malath is the first insurer checked whose public copy uses the word **classes**. That is
notable but weaker than it looks:

| Question | Answered by Malath publicly? |
|---|---|
| How many classes exist? | **No** |
| What are they called? | **No** |
| Is one of them "Class A" or "Class B"? | **No** |
| Which providers are in each class? | **No** |
| What benefits does each class include? | **No** ("upgraded benefits for higher classes", unquantified) |

**Do not conclude that Malath's "classes" are the Class A / Class B on a card.** The word
appears once, in a marketing sentence, with no list attached. It is consistent with the
market vocabulary and worth mentioning to the user as a lead — but it is not a mapping, and
the skill must not treat it as one.

## Group health

Malath sells group health to companies as a single contract, with in-patient, out-patient,
or comprehensive scope. As with Bupa Corporate and Tawuniya's corporate product, the network
comes from the employer's contract — so two people both holding "Malath" can be on different
networks. Say so if the user is on a company plan rather than an SME plan.
