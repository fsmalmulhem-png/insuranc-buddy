# SAICO — plans, categories, and what is publicly known

Populated from SAICO's own site (`saico.com.sa`), read 11 November 2026.

## Who they are

**الشركة السعودية للتأمين التعاوني** — Saudi Arabian Cooperative Insurance Company (SAICO).
The site lists motor, travel, medical, property, engineering, marine cargo, liability, cyber,
life and SME bundles.

## The health products — two, both employer-facing

| Product | Page | Audience |
|---|---|---|
| **Medical** | `/service/medical/` | **SMEs** — "designed our medical insurance products specifically for SMEs in the Kingdom" |
| **Medical** | `/service/medical-2/` | **Corporates** — "comprehensive medical insurance solutions that meet your needs" |

Both pages describe the same benefits in the same words: hospitalization, consultations,
surgeries, emergency treatments, plus "dental and optical cover, wellness programs, and
telemedicine services".

**Neither page lists a tier, a category, a class, or a single hospital.** Each ends by inviting
the reader to use a contact form.

Notably, **SAICO appears to sell no individual medical product** — both health pages address
businesses. Its individual menu covers motor, travel, home, personal accident, medical
malpractice and visitors-visa extension.

## The "category and network" finding

SAICO's FAQ, in the medical cash-reimbursement section, says:

> "…the policyholder shall be compensated… according to the prevailing rates for the
> **category and network** of the customer…"

and separately:

> "For non-emergency cases, **prior approval from SAICO is required**."

This is worth extracting for the write-up, because it is the market behaviour described in one
sentence by an insurer itself:

- SAICO **knows** there are customer **categories** and customer **networks** — different
  members are entitled to different things.
- SAICO **does not publish** what the categories are, what they contain, or which providers
  belong to which.

That is the project's thesis, stated by the insurer rather than by us. Quote it.

SAICO joins the "nothing published" group — but on the class question it is the most useful
company in the set, because it is the only one that **acknowledges in writing** that members
carry different categories and networks. It publishes the least *data* and the most *admission*.
That distinction belongs in the write-up: the evidence that coverage is class-level does not come
only from what insurers publish, but also from what they say while publishing nothing.
