# "Integrated Insurance" — a domain that does not exist, and a broker behind it

## Status — two separate findings

**1. The URL supplied does not exist.** `integratedinsurance.com.sa` has **no DNS record**, and the
Wayback Machine has **zero captures** of it — not one, ever. It is not a dead site; it never existed.

**2. The real entity is a broker, not an insurer.** The phrase "integrated insurance" is marketing
copy from **NIB — National Insurance Brokerage** (`nib.com.sa`): *"a licensed Saudi insurance
brokerage company providing customized insurance coverage."*

A **broker** sits between the customer and an insurer. It has **no provider network**, **no classes**
and **no coverage of its own**. Every question this project answers is a question for the
**underlying insurer**, which the broker does not disclose publicly.

Verified 11 November 2026. See `sources/index.md`.

---

## Why this matters more than it looks

Everything in this project assumes the user names an **insurer**. A broker breaks that assumption:

- The member's card is issued by the **insurer**, not the broker.
- The network belongs to the **insurer**.
- The class belongs to the **insurer's** policy.

If a user says "I have Integrated Insurance" and their policy actually came through NIB, then the
right next question is **"which insurance company is printed on your card?"** — not "Class A or B".

**This is the first entity in the set for which the correct first answer is "I need a different
company name," not a network answer.**

## What NIB is — verified from its own site

**National Insurance Brokerage** — licensed by the **Insurance Authority** (Saudi regulator).

| Claim, in its own words |
|---|
| "A licensed Saudi **insurance brokerage** company" |
| "Licensed by the Insurance Authority" |
| "We work with more than **20** leading insurance companies" |
| "**15+** Years of Experience" |
| "providing insurance coverage … through most licensed insurance companies in the Saudi market" |

So NIB places business with 20+ insurers. **It does not underwrite.** Which insurer a given member
is with depends entirely on which of those 20+ the policy was placed with.

### Products NIB places (from its own description)

Vehicle · **Medical** · Marine · and more — as a broker, it offers whatever its partner insurers
sell.

## What is NOT available from a broker

| Question | Answerable from NIB? |
|---|---|
| Which hospitals accept my policy? | **No** — the insurer's network governs |
| What class am I? | **No** — the insurer's policy sets it |
| Which insurer am I with? | **Only from the member's own documents** |
| Is NIB an insurer? | **No** — a licensed broker |

NIB's site lists no provider network, no class structure, and no insurer-by-policy lookup. None of
that is a failure on NIB's part — a broker does not hold that data.

## What the agent must do when someone says "Integrated Insurance"

1. **Tell them the domain does not exist.** If they are following a URL, it is wrong. Do not invent
   content for it.
2. **Ask what is printed on their card or policy.** The important question is **which insurance
   company** issued it. A broker's name on correspondence is not the insurer.
3. **If they name a broker** (NIB or similar), explain plainly: a broker arranges cover but does not
   provide the network. The network and class come from the insurer behind the policy.
4. **Once they name an actual insurer**, use that insurer's skill — this project has skills for
   Bupa, Amana, AICC, GIG, Tawuniya, MedGulf, Al Sagr, Aljazira Takaful, Al Rajhi Takaful, Walaa,
   Salama, SAICO, ACIG, UCA, Gulf Union, and others.
5. **Do not name a hospital** and do not discuss classes for a broker name — there is nothing to
   answer.
6. If they need to reach NIB: **8001240284**.

### The answer block — use this shape every time

1. **What they hold:** a policy placed **through a broker**; the insurer is unknown.
2. **Class:** ask for Class A / Class B — but it belongs to the insurer's policy, not the broker's.
3. **What to do next:** read the **insurer's name** off the card. Then use that insurer's skill.
4. **Always ask the hospital:** *"Do you bill [insurer] Class [A/B] directly?"* — using the insurer's
   name, never the broker's.
