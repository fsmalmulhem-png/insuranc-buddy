## The RIADI product

MEDGULF's health insurance for businesses of **up to 250 employees** is sold as **RIADI**.
MEDGULF describes it as five distinct insurance categories, each with a different network
depth, copay structure and add-ons.

All five rows are from the same page, listed in this order, each with its own icon and its

### Reading the price column honestly

These are MEDGULF's displayed "Starting from" values including VAT. MEDGULF does not state on
that page what the price is per — per employee per year is the conventional reading for a
Saudi SME health product, but the page does not say so explicitly, and it does not state
whether the figure covers one person or a family.

**Do not quote these prices to a user as a cost estimate.** This skill's role excludes cost
quotes. The price column exists so the ordering can be seen and traced, not so it can be
repeated as a premium.

### Reading the provider count honestly

The counts are MEDGULF's own "+ N Providers" claims for each tier. They are:

- **Totals, with no city breakdown.** A member in a small town cannot tell whether any of
  the 300 Basic providers are near them.
- **Not names.** No hospital or clinic is listed under any tier anywhere on the site.
- **Not proven nested.** The counts rise with tier and the descriptions suggest increasing
  breadth, but MEDGULF never states that Tier X includes everything in Tier Y. Treat the
  ordering as a description of network size, not as a containment guarantee.

MEDGULF's public pages never use "Class A" or "Class B". A member holding a card that prints
one of those two words cannot be placed into a RIADI tier from public information. Do not
invent a mapping, and do not assume Class A is the higher of the two — the skill does not
know, and neither does the site.
