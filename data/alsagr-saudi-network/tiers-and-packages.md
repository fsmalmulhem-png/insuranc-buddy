# Al Sagr — plans, classes, and who actually holds the network

Populated from Al Sagr's own site (`alsagr.com`), read 11 November 2026.

## The health product

Al Sagr's FAQ treats health insurance as an established product and answers three member
questions about it:

| Question on Al Sagr's FAQ | Their answer, in substance |
|---|---|
| What does medical insurance cost? | Varies by gender, age, nationality, medical status and geographic coverage limit. Get a quote on the retail portal, at a sales point, or on **920001043** (WhatsApp available) |
| **How do I enquire about the medical network?** | **Via TCS — see below** |
| Can I cancel medical insurance? | Yes — email `Cancellation.Medical@alsagr.com` with documents via their file-share form; cancellation completes within 60 working days |

The retail e-commerce portal sells Motor, Travel, Property and Medical Malpractice — **not**
individual health. So health is an employer/group product in practice; the retail portal does
not offer it to a walk-in buyer.

## Classes — and the one unusual answer

| Aspect | Published by Al Sagr? |
|---|---|
| Number of classes | **No** |
| Class names | **No** |
| Class A / Class B mapping | **No** |
| Class → provider mapping | **No** |
| Provider list (any format) | **No** |
| **How a member finds their own network** | **Yes — published in full** (TCS / Alsagr Health app / TCS portal) |

Al Sagr is identical to the other six on the first five rows. It is unique on the sixth: it
tells the member who holds the network and how to ask them.

## The structural point

The network is held by **TCS**, the medical claims administrator (TPA). Al Sagr sells the
policy; TCS administers claims and the provider network. That split is common in Saudi health
insurance and it explains why Al Sagr's own site carries no provider list — the data lives
with its administrator.

For the project's write-up this is important: "does this insurer publish its network" is not
always the right question. For Al Sagr the right question is "does the *administrator* expose
it", and the answer is yes — through a member lookup, not a file.
